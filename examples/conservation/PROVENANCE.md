# Conservation data: provenance

This directory's data files are generated by `scripts/conservation/build.mjs`.
They hold real per-residue conservation scores for rubredoxin from
_Pyrococcus furiosus_ (UniProt **P24297**, RUBR_PYRFU, 54 aa). The scores come
from the **Pfam PF00301 "Rubredoxin" full alignment**. Do not edit the files by
hand: `scripts/conservation/score.spec.mjs` checks their sha256 values against
`provenance.json`.

| File                  | What it holds                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `conservation.csv`    | `position,value`: one score for each of residues 4–49, to 3 decimal places.                                                                          |
| `conserved-sites.csv` | `type,start,end,description,score`: the most conserved residues as `SITE` features, each with its score.                                             |
| `provenance.json`     | The machine-readable record: sources, releases, retrieval dates, sha256 values of inputs and outputs, method parameters, and the sanity check below. |
| `config.yaml`         | The viewer config (written by hand, not generated): the line graph, the most conserved residues, and UniProt's binding sites.                        |

## Method

The score is the **Jensen–Shannon divergence** (JSD) of each alignment column
from the BLOSUM62 background distribution (Capra & Singh 2007). It uses
Henikoff position-based sequence weights and a gap penalty, with no window
smoothing.

- **Columns.** Only match states are used (upper case or `-` in hmmalign's
  output). Insert columns are dropped.
- **Sequence weights.** Henikoff & Henikoff 1994, computed over the match
  columns. Gaps gain no weight and do not count as a residue type.
- **Column distribution.**
  - Each of the 20 amino acids and the gap gets a pseudocount of 1e-7, plus the
    weights of the rows holding it.
  - The gap is then dropped and the 20 counts are normalised to sum to 1.
  - B counts as D and Z as Q. X, and any other letter, counts as a gap.
- **Score.** JSD (base 2) between the column distribution and the background,
  multiplied by (1 − the weighted fraction of gaps in the column).
- **Gap cutoff.** A column where more than 30% of rows (unweighted) are gaps is
  not scored.
- **Background.** These are the published values from Capra & Singh's
  `distributions/blosum62.distribution`, in the order A R N D C Q E G H I L K M
  F P S T W Y V:

  `0.078 0.051 0.041 0.052 0.024 0.034 0.059 0.083 0.025 0.062 0.092 0.056 0.024 0.044 0.043 0.059 0.055 0.014 0.034 0.072`

  The file is in `conservation_code.tar.gz` from
  <https://compbio.cs.princeton.edu/conservation/> (sha256
  `ee2565eb93a1e8865fdfab544b70c0b2b6199c86f81b737e17ef0f16bf2fe26c`).
  - As published, the values sum to **1.002**, because each is rounded to 3 dp.
  - They are used **as published, not renormalised**. The reference program
    does the same: it accepts any background summing to between 0.997 and
    1.003.
  - Renormalising would lower each scored column by 0.0001 to 0.0008 and leave
    the ranking unchanged. For example, an invariant C column would score
    0.9180 instead of 0.9189.

- **Following the reference program.** Where the paper leaves a detail open,
  the method follows its reference program, `score_conservation.py`: how gaps
  are weighted, the pseudocount, and the B/Z/X handling. `score.mjs` is an
  independent implementation; none of that program's GPL code is copied, only
  the published background values.
- **Mapping to residues.**
  - P24297 is a row of the alignment, `RUBR_PYRFU/3-49`, so no aligner is
    needed.
  - Residue numbers are counted from the row's `/start`, and inserts are
    counted.
  - The generator checks that the row's residues are identical to the UniProt
    sequence, and fails if they are not.
- **Most conserved residues.**
  - A residue is included if its score is at least the value at ascending
    index ⌊0.9 × (n − 1)⌋ among the n scored residues. Adjacent residues are
    merged into runs.
  - This is "at or above the 90th percentile". Ties can make the set larger than
    10%: here it is 6 of 46.
  - The rule is applied to the published 3 dp values, so `conserved-sites.csv`
    can be re-derived exactly from `conservation.csv`.

## Sanity check

UniProt annotates four Fe-binding sites on P24297 (Binding site, "Fe cation"):
cysteines 6, 9, 39 and 42. They are the **top four** of the 46 scored residues.

| Residue    | Score | Rank |
| ---------- | ----- | ---- |
| C6         | 0.877 | 1    |
| C39        | 0.862 | 2    |
| C42        | 0.844 | 3    |
| C9         | 0.821 | 4    |
| Y13 (next) | 0.781 | 5    |

The six most conserved residues are C6, C9, Y13, W37, C39 and C42. They make
up the aromatic and iron-binding core of the fold.

## Reading the numbers

- The scores measure conservation **within the Pfam rubredoxin domain
  family**. They are not a whole-protein evolutionary rate.
- JSD rewards rare residues. An invariant C or W outscores an invariant L,
  because C and W are rarer in the background.
- Residues 1–3 and 50–54 lie outside the Pfam domain, so they have no score.
  The line covers 4–49 without a break.
- The numbers belong to P24297. On another protein's sequence they mean
  nothing, even when they are in range.

## Regenerating and checking

```sh
node scripts/conservation/build.mjs            # PF00301 and P24297 by default; rewrites the three files
node scripts/conservation/build.mjs --check    # writes nothing; exits 1 on any difference
```

The generator uses the network, so it runs by hand and never in CI.

- **`--check`.** It recomputes everything from today's data and compares the
  two CSVs line by line, and every field of `provenance.json`. It names the
  first difference.
- **`retrieved`.** The `retrieved` fields come from each response's `Date`
  header, so `--check` ignores them. Otherwise a re-run on another day would
  always differ.
- **New releases.** After a new Pfam or UniProt release, `--check` may report
  drift: new values, a changed alignment sha256, or a missing row. The committed
  files remain the authoritative record. Regenerating them is a deliberate change
  that should be reviewed.

The unit tests (`scripts/conservation/score.spec.mjs`) run offline on
`scripts/conservation/__fixtures__/PF00301.seed.sto`:

- It is the PF00301 **seed** alignment: 21 sequences, 3,311 bytes, sha256
  `079fb1456260bde9a6de959326640abd74a3fae6fec92911e290ee2844688174`.
- It was retrieved on 2026-10-05 from
  `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/api/entry/pfam/PF00301/?annotation=alignment:seed`
  (Pfam 38.2).
- It contains `RUBR_PYRFU/3-49`. On the seed, the four Fe-binding cysteines tie
  for the top score (0.919), and Y13 comes next (0.893).
